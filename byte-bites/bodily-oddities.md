### <a id="alice-in-wonderland-syndrome" href="#alice-in-wonderland-syndrome" class="pattern-title-link">Alice in Wonderland syndrome</a> <a href="https://vester.si/bodily-oddities/oddities/alice-in-wonderland-syndrome/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="pattern-tweet-btn" title="View entry on Bodily Oddities">↗</a>

**Prevalence**: 6–7% of teenagers (size distortion) · **Kind**: Perception · **System**: Brain, Eyes & Vision

*Also known as: AIWS, Todd's syndrome, Micropsia and macropsia, Tachysensia, Fast feeling*

Objects, body parts, or the whole room suddenly look far too big, far too small, or far away. It mostly happens to children, often during a fever or infection, and it passes on its own. In adults it is often linked to migraine.

*Also known as: True ambidexterity*

Using both hands equally well for every task. About 1% of people are truly ambidextrous. Many people who say they are ambidextrous are mixed-handed: right hand for some jobs, left for others.

### <a id="aphantasia" href="#aphantasia" class="pattern-title-link">Aphantasia</a> <a href="https://vester.si/bodily-oddities/oddities/aphantasia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="pattern-tweet-btn" title="View entry on Bodily Oddities">↗</a>

**Prevalence**: About 1% have no mental images. About 4% have faint or none. · **Kind**: Perception · **System**: Brain

*Also known as: Congenital aphantasia*

Most people see a picture in their mind when they think of a friend's face. People with aphantasia see only a faint image or none. About 4 in 100 people are like this, and about 1 in 100 see nothing at all. A few people have images almost as vivid as real sight.

🏷️ `try-it` `genetic` `famous-people` `community`

### <a id="asmr" href="#asmr" class="pattern-title-link">ASMR</a> <a href="https://vester.si/bodily-oddities/oddities/asmr/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="pattern-tweet-btn" title="View entry on Bodily Oddities">↗</a>

**Prevalence**: Unclear; estimates 20–60% · **Kind**: Involuntary · **System**: Brain, Skin & Hair

*Also known as: Autonomous sensory meridian response, "Brain tingles"*

A pleasant tingling that starts on the scalp and moves down the neck and spine, set off by whispering, tapping, soft sounds, or gentle personal attention. Not everyone gets it. A large online community is built around it.

🏷️ `community`

### <a id="brain-zaps" href="#brain-zaps" class="pattern-title-link">Brain zaps</a> <a href="https://vester.si/bodily-oddities/oddities/brain-zaps/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="pattern-tweet-btn" title="View entry on Bodily Oddities">↗</a>

**Prevalence**: 15–50% of people stopping an antidepressant get withdrawal symptoms · **Kind**: Involuntary · **System**: Brain, Nervous System & Reflexes

*Also known as: Brain shivers, Brain shocks, Electrical shock sensations*

A brief feeling like a small electric shock inside the head, often set off by moving the eyes. It is most common when stopping or lowering an antidepressant. It is unpleasant, but usually not a problem.

🏷️ `not-an-emergency`

### <a id="call-of-the-void" href="#call-of-the-void" class="pattern-title-link">Call of the void</a> <a href="https://vester.si/bodily-oddities/oddities/call-of-the-void/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="pattern-tweet-btn" title="View entry on Bodily Oddities">↗</a>

**Prevalence**: 43–60% of people · **Kind**: Involuntary · **System**: Brain

*Also known as: L'appel du vide, High place phenomenon*

A sudden, brief thought of jumping when you stand somewhere high, with no wish to do it. About half of people get it. It is probably the brain's safety system being misread, not a hidden wish.

🏷️ `not-an-emergency`

### <a id="charles-bonnet-syndrome" href="#charles-bonnet-syndrome" class="pattern-title-link">Charles Bonnet syndrome</a> <a href="https://vester.si/bodily-oddities/oddities/charles-bonnet-syndrome/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="pattern-tweet-btn" title="View entry on Bodily Oddities">↗</a>

**Prevalence**: 10–40% of older people with serious sight loss · **Kind**: Perception · **System**: Brain, Eyes & Vision

*Also known as: CBS, Visual release hallucinations*

People who lose a lot of their sight often start to see things that are not there: patterns, faces, small people in costume, whole landscapes. They know the images are not real. It is caused by sight loss, not by mental illness.

🏷️ `not-an-emergency` `illusion`

Glance at a clock and the second hand can seem to freeze for a moment before it moves. The brain hides your eye movement and stretches the first thing you see to cover the gap.

Seeing, hearing, or even reading about a yawn makes many adults yawn. The effect appears in early childhood, may be linked to empathy, and crosses over to dogs.

The world suddenly seems flat, distant, or like a film (derealization), or you feel like you are watching yourself from outside (depersonalization). Many people have a brief episode at some point, usually under stress.

*Also known as: Photographic memory (popular term)*

Some children can keep a detailed picture of an image in view for a short time after it is taken away. The ability fades by the teenage years. A true photographic memory in adults has never been confirmed.

*Also known as: Music chills, Aesthetic chills*

A wave of chills up the back, sometimes with goosebumps, at a powerful moment in music, a film, or a speech. It is thought to come from the brain's reward system reacting to an expectation being met or broken.

Fill your whole field of view with a featureless colour and, after some minutes, your brain may start inventing shapes, lights, and scenes. Two halved ping-pong balls and a lamp are enough to do it at home.

### <a id="hsam" href="#hsam" class="pattern-title-link">Highly superior autobiographical memory</a> <a href="https://vester.si/bodily-oddities/oddities/hsam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="pattern-tweet-btn" title="View entry on Bodily Oddities">↗</a>

*Also known as: HSAM, Hyperthymesia*

Fewer than a hundred people worldwide have been confirmed to recall what happened on almost any date since about age 10, in detail and without effort. Their memory for other things, such as lists or numbers, is ordinary.

### <a id="human-echolocation" href="#human-echolocation" class="pattern-title-link">Human echolocation</a> <a href="https://vester.si/bodily-oddities/oddities/human-echolocation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="pattern-tweet-btn" title="View entry on Bodily Oddities">↗</a>

*Also known as: Flash sonar, Click echolocation*

Some blind people find their way by making sharp tongue clicks and listening to the echoes. Brain scans show the echoes are processed in the visual cortex. Sighted people can learn a basic version in ten weeks of training.

### <a id="migraine-aura" href="#migraine-aura" class="pattern-title-link">Migraine aura</a> <a href="https://vester.si/bodily-oddities/oddities/migraine-aura/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="pattern-tweet-btn" title="View entry on Bodily Oddities">↗</a>

A change in vision that builds over minutes and usually fades within an hour. The best-known form is a shimmering zigzag, but auras vary a lot: flashes, fog, blind patches or static. It can come before a migraine, or alone.

**Prevalence**: About 1% of Northern Europeans (two copies) · **Kind**: Rare Ability · **System**: Whole Body & Genetics, Heart & Blood

*Also known as: CCR5-Δ32, CCR5 delta 32 deletion*

About 1% of people of Northern European descent carry two copies of a broken gene that makes them almost immune to the most common form of HIV. A bone marrow transplant from one such donor produced the first cure of HIV.

**Prevalence**: Unknown. Fewer than 100 documented cases · **Kind**: Rare Ability · **System**: Whole Body & Genetics

*Also known as: Tetragametic chimerism, Human chimera*

A chimera is one person made of two sets of cells with different DNA. It can happen when two embryos fuse in the first days of pregnancy. Most chimeras never find out. A few discovered it when a DNA test said they were not the mother of their own children.
